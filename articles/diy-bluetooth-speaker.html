How to Build a Custom 30W Bluetooth Speaker (TPA3110 + 12V Li-Ion Guide)

By Corelume Tech Editorial Team • November 28, 2026 • 6 Min Read • DIY Audio Engineering

Off-the-shelf portable speakers trade acoustic volume and repairability for glued-together plastic shells. When you build your own Bluetooth speaker from discrete modules, you control the enclosure volume, driver sensitivity, power rail stability, and thermal headroom.

This guide walks through a proven, repeatable 2 × 15W (30W total) stereo build powered by a 12V lithium-ion pack. It avoids the classic beginner mistakes—power-supply whine, brownouts on bass transients, and starved Bluetooth receiver rails.

System Architecture & Power Budget

At full tilt into 4-ohm loads, a dual-channel 15W Class-D stage draws roughly 30W of acoustic power. Accounting for ~88% Class-D efficiency, peak input draw sits near 34W (~2.8A at 12V). Music has a crest factor, so average draw is much lower, but your power bus must handle 3A+ instantaneous peaks without voltage sag.

To keep digital Bluetooth noise out of the analog input stage, we split the 12V bus:

  1. Main Amplifier Rail (12V Direct): Feeds the Class-D output stage directly from the battery BMS.
  2. Regulated Logic Rail (5V Buck): Steps 12V down to a clean 5V for indicator LEDs, auxiliary USB charging, or a standalone 5V Bluetooth receiver.

What You Need (Parts BOM)

Every component below is selected for electrical compatibility and thermal margin. Swap parts only if you verify voltage and current ratings.

  • TPA3110 Amplifier: A 2 × 15W Class-D stereo amplifier board (look for variants with integrated Bluetooth 5.0 or pair with a dedicated BT receiver). Operates cleanly from 8V to 24V DC.
  • 12V Lithium-Ion Pack: A 3S (11.1V nominal, 12.6V peak) lithium-ion battery pack with an integrated 10A+ BMS for over-discharge and short-circuit protection.
  • LM2596 Voltage Regulator: Adjustable DC-DC buck converter to step 12.6V down to a rock-solid 5.0V for auxiliary logic, status LEDs, or an external Bluetooth receiver.
  • Drivers (×2): 3-inch or 4-inch full-range 4Ω speakers rated for 15W–25W RMS.
  • Enclosure: 12mm Baltic birch plywood or 4mm-wall PETG/ABS 3D-printed shell (~2.5L to 3.5L internal volume per driver, sealed or ported).
  • Hardware: SPST latching power switch (rated 5A+), DC barrel charging jack (5.5 × 2.1mm), 18 AWG silicone wire, and acoustic polyfill.

Step 1: Dial In the LM2596 Buck Converter First

Never connect a load to an uncalibrated buck converter. Adjustable LM2596 boards often ship set to pass input voltage straight through or sit at an arbitrary 9V.

  1. Wire the 12V Lithium-Ion Pack positive and negative leads to IN+ and IN- on the LM2596 Voltage Regulator.
  2. Probe OUT+ and OUT- with a multimeter set to DC volts.
  3. Turn the brass potentiometer screw counter-clockwise (often 10–15 turns) until the meter locks onto 5.00V.
  4. Add a dab of hot glue or nail polish to the pot screw so speaker vibrations don't drift your voltage rail.

Step 2: Star-Ground Wiring & Power Bus

Daisy-chaining ground wires between a high-current Class-D amp and a sensitive 5V logic rail creates a ground loop—heard as a high-pitched whine whenever Bluetooth is searching or audio is paused. Use a star-ground topology where all negative returns meet at one physical node right at the battery/switch.

[12V Li-Ion Pack + BMS]
   │
   ├──(+)──[ 5A Main Switch ]──┬──(+)──► [TPA3110 Amplifier VCC]
   │                           │
   │                           └──(+)──► [LM2596 IN+] ──(5.0V OUT+)──► [BT / LEDs]
   │
   └──(-)──[ STAR GROUND NODE ]──┬──(-)──► [TPA3110 Amplifier GND]
                                 │
                                 └──(-)──► [LM2596 IN- / OUT-]

[TPA3110 Output]
   ├── L+ / L- ──(18 AWG)──► Left 4Ω Full-Range Driver
   └── R+ / R- ──(18 AWG)──► Right 4Ω Full-Range Driver

Wiring Checklist

  • Keep speaker leads (L+/L-, R+/R-) twisted together to minimize EMI radiation.
  • Never bridge L- and R- together on a TPA3110—Class-D outputs are BTL (Bridge-Tied Load) and both terminals are actively driven. Shorting them to ground or each other will trip protection or fry the IC.
  • Place a 1000µF / 25V low-ESR electrolytic capacitor across the TPA3110 VCC and GND pads if you hear clipping on heavy kick drums.

Step 3: Enclosure Volume & Acoustic Sealing

An unbaffled speaker sounds thin because rear pressure waves cancel front waves (acoustic dipole cancellation).

  • Sealed vs. Ported: A sealed box stuffed loosely with acoustic polyfill (~50% fill) gives tight transient response and protects small drivers from over-excursion below resonance.
  • Airtight Joints: Run a bead of wood glue (or silicone for 3D prints) along every internal seam. Use closed-cell EVA foam gaskets under the speaker frames and terminal cups. Any air leak creates audible chuffing at high volume.

Step 4: Bench Test Before Final Assembly

Before bolting the rear panel shut:

  1. Power on with no audio playing and put your ear 5 cm from the cone. You should hear only a faint thermal hiss—no clicking or whine.
  2. Pair via Bluetooth and sweep a 40Hz–18kHz tone at 50% volume to catch panel rattles or air leaks early.
  3. Monitor the TPA3110 heatsink and LM2596 inductor after 15 minutes at 75% volume; both should stay warm to the touch, not scorching.

Once verified, seal the backplate, charge the 3S pack to 12.6V with a dedicated 12.6V CC/CV Li-ion charger, and enjoy clean, repairable 30W wireless audio.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Why use an LM2596 buck converter with a TPA3110 amplifier?

The TPA3110 runs directly off a 12V 3S lithium-ion battery pack, while external Bluetooth receivers, status LEDs, and auxiliary logic need a regulated 5.0V rail. Using an LM2596 buck converter prevents over-voltage damage and isolates logic power.

Can I bridge L- and R- speaker outputs on a TPA3110 board?

No. TPA3110 outputs are Bridge-Tied Load (BTL), meaning both positive and negative terminals are actively driven. Connecting L- and R- together or to ground will trigger protection or damage the amplifier IC.

How do I stop Bluetooth ground-loop whine in a DIY speaker?

Use a star-ground wiring topology where the TPA3110 ground and LM2596 ground meet at a single physical node near the battery BMS negative terminal rather than daisy-chaining ground wires.

What battery pack is best for a 30W TPA3110 Bluetooth speaker?

A 12V 3S lithium-ion battery pack (11.1V nominal, 12.6V full charge) with an integrated 10A+ BMS handles 3A+ bass transient peaks without voltage sag or premature brownouts.
